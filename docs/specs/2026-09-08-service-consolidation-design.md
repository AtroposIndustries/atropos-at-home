# Atropos — service consolidation

**Date:** 2026-09-08
**Branch:** `restructure-services`
**Status:** implemented on `restructure-services`; service list, route table, nav content and stub map superseded by `2026-09-13-nav-and-offerings-design.md`
**Supersedes in part:** `2026-08-25-atropos-dual-vertical-design.md` (its service

## Context

The dual-vertical rebrand shipped with thirteen services across twenty routes.
It read as dense — "trying to do too much" — and a competitor benchmark made
the reason concrete. Connected Audio Visual (Sydney), whose offering the
business wants to match, lists six residential and six commercial services;
minus the security lines Atropos cannot offer, that is **four and three**. The
Melbourne firm closest to our shape lists six and four. Cyberhomes, nineteen
years in, lists eleven total with commercial AV as a single line item.

Ours was six and seven, with six of the seven commercial services mirroring a
residential one. The mirroring is what produced the impression of a menu
nobody could staff.

This document collapses the offering to match the benchmark, keeping two
deliberate departures from it.

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|----------|-----|
| 1 | Acoustic treatment folds into Home Theatre as a prominent section, on both sides | Matches the benchmark, which never lists it standalone. Keeps the keywords and the expertise; loses two menu items |
| 2 | Residential support dissolves into an aftercare section on every page; **commercial Managed Services stays standalone** | Contracted monitoring and response is the clearest thing our commercial side says that the benchmark does not. Their omission is a gap in their offering, not a virtue, and it is where recurring revenue lives |
| 3 | One Smart Home Automation page, not the benchmark's two | Their Smart Home and Automation pages overlap almost entirely — their own content cannot tell them apart. Copying a competitor's muddle because they have it is the wrong lesson |
| 4 | Keep the Residential / Commercial nav dropdowns | The benchmark's nav (Our Work / About / Blog / Contact) works because a portfolio does the selling. We have no portfolio and no blog. With 3–4 services a side the dropdowns are short, not dense. Revisit when there is work to show |
| 5 | No security services, anywhere | Not security-certified. CCTV, surveillance, access control and intercom are out on both sides. Where a page mentions them it is as **integration with the client's existing provider**, never as something we supply |
| 6 | Surviving slugs keep their URLs; merged content gets one new slug | Renaming a surviving page costs a redirect stub for nothing. The one genuinely new page — commercial Audio Visual — absorbs three old ones and takes a new slug that describes what it now covers |

are courtesy to crawlers more than to people.

## Copy

Each surviving page follows the existing shape — hero, intro, features, CTA,
contact form — with two additions taken from the benchmark:

**An aftercare section on every service page.** This is how residential support
dissolves without disappearing: every page says what happens after handover.
Concrete, no response-time figures, no adverbs of frequency about work already
done.

**Absorbed content becomes named sections, not bullets.** Acoustic treatment
inside Home Theatre gets a heading and a paragraph, not a line in a list. The
whole point of folding it is that it stays visible.

**Pages will be longer than they are now.** The benchmark runs 850–1,300 words a
page; ours are 700–900 after the tone pass. A page absorbing two others will
grow naturally. Do not pad to match the benchmark — its length is carried by
proof we do not have.

**The standing content rules are unchanged** and are restated in the tone brief
used for every rewrite: no commercial track record claimed or implied, no
adverbs of frequency about Atropos's own commercial work, no invented SLA or
uptime figures, no unverifiable standards or legal claims, no individuals, no
smallness signals, brand is "Atropos".

**Security language.** Smart Home Automation currently has a "Security &
Access" feature describing cameras, locks and alarms as something we install.
It becomes integration: the control system works with the security provider
the client already has. The same applies anywhere else security appears.

## What we deliberately do not copy

**Their proof.** Control4 Diamond status, Dealer of the Year, "300+ clients",
six named business testimonials. Their page length is carried by that. Ours
is carried by specificity, and only that.

**Their Smart Home / Automation split.** Decision 3.

**Their nav.** Decision 4.

**Their length.** See Copy.

## Testing

Existing guards stay and their assertions move: three residential services,
four commercial. The filesystem-parity test enforces that every removed
directory is gone and every declared route has a page. The form-page guard
enforces that all eleven form-bearing pages are declared.

The Zoho Form Location URL is a wildcard (`LAUNCH.md` §7), so no Zoho change is
needed for the URL changes. The removed pages need nothing on the Zoho side
either.

## External configuration

- **Zoho** — nothing. The wildcard covers new and removed URLs alike.
- **Search Console** — the sitemap regenerates from `lib/routes.js`; resubmit
  after merge so the removed URLs drop out of Google's queue sooner.
- **`atroposathome-redirect`** — three of its stubs (`audio`, `acoustic`,
  `support`) point at `/residential/*` pages this change removes, so they would
  become two-hop chains again. Re-point them at `/residential/home-theatre/`,
  `/residential/home-theatre/` and `/residential/` respectively, **after** this
  branch merges so the destinations exist.
